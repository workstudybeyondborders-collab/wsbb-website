## The Two Program Options

Denmark offers two main pathways for qualified Nigerians: Agriculture and Hospitality. Both are paid, structured, and 18 months long.

![African female veterinarian gently examining a piglet inside a clean Danish farm pen](/images/uploads/Vet.Doctor.png)

### Agriculture Internship

You will work full-time (37 hours per week) on a modern Danish farm. Think dairy, pig, greenhouse, or crop farms. Daily tasks include animal care, milking, feeding, operating farm machinery, planting, and harvesting. Danish farms are high-tech and sustainability-focused, so you walk away with skills that are relevant anywhere in the world. This path is ideal if you studied agriculture, have some farm experience, or simply enjoy hands-on physical work outdoors.

### Hospitality Internship

You will be placed in hotels, restaurants, cafes, or hospitality businesses across Denmark. Duties range from front-desk service and guest relations to kitchen support and housekeeping management. Denmark's tourism and hospitality sector is a significant part of the economy, employing both Danish and international staff year-round. This option suits those with a background in hotel management, tourism, or catering, and people who genuinely enjoy working with people. In both programs, you get structured on-the-job training, not just classroom theory.

## What Is Included in the Package

Going to Denmark through this program is not just about a salary. It comes with a full package that makes your monthly earnings go much further:

- **Accommodation** : Your host employer arranges housing at a fraction of the market rate, typically DKK 1,000 to 3,000 per month (roughly ₦160,000 to ₦480,000). You get a private room with basic amenities like internet and a kitchen. Compare that to paying Lagos rent on a Lagos salary.
- **Free Healthcare**: Once you arrive and complete your CPR registration (more on this below), you are automatically enrolled in Denmark's public healthcare system. That means free doctor visits, hospital treatment, and emergency care, all covered by the Danish state.
- **Paid Sick Leave**: If you fall ill, you still get paid. That is not a benefit most Nigerian employers offer.
- **5 Weeks Paid Vacation:** Every year, you earn five weeks of paid leave, plus a mandatory 12.5% holiday allowance paid out separately each May.
- **9% Flex-Account Contribution**: Your employer contributes 9% of your salary into a flex savings account you can access for approved needs.
- **Standard Workweek**: 37 hours per week. Weekends are protected, and overtime is compensated.

## What Is a CPR Number?

You will hear this term a lot in Denmark, so let us explain it clearly. CPR stands for Det Centrale Personregister, which is Denmark's Central Person Register. It is a 10-digit personal identification number that every person living in Denmark must have. Think of it like Nigeria's NIN, but far more powerful. Your CPR number unlocks everything: healthcare, your bank account, tax registration, and your legal residency status. Without it, you essentially do not exist in the Danish system.

You get your CPR number by visiting your local government office (called a Borgerservice or Citizen Service Centre) within your first week of arriving in Denmark. You go in person, bring your residence permit and passport, and they register you on the spot. After that, your healthcare kicks in automatically and you can open a Danish bank account.

## Who Qualifies?

Not everyone will qualify for every program, and at WSBB we would rather tell you the truth upfront than waste your time. Here is what you typically need:

- **Age**: 18 to 30 years old for agriculture; up to 35 for hospitality
- **Education or experience**: You are a current student of an agricultural educational institution of any zootechnical specialty in your country (diploma, degree, or practical farm experience) for the agriculture track; hotel, restaurant, or tourism studies or work experience for hospitality
- **Language**: Functional English is required. You have a KET, TOEFL, or IELTS certificate with at least 3.0/A2 level.  Most Danish employers and colleagues speak excellent English
- **Valid passport**: With enough validity to cover the full 18-month program plus some buffer
- **Clean record**: No criminal history that would affect a Danish residence permit application
- **Genuine motivation**: Danish employers value interns who are adaptable, hardworking, and ready to learn. This is a training program, not just a job

### The Visa and Permit Process (Simplified)

We know the word "visa" makes most people nervous. Here is the honest truth: the process is very manageable when someone walks you through it step by step. This is exactly what we do at WSBB.

1. **Apply and Get Accepted**: You apply through WSBB, we assess your profile, and if you qualify, we match you with a verified Danish host farm or hotel. They issue you a formal internship contract.
2. **Submit Your Residence Permit Application**: Using that contract, we help you submit your application to SIRI (Denmark's Agency for International Recruitment and Integration), the official Danish government body that handles this permit.
3. **Pay the Permit Fee**: The current government fee for an internship residence permit is approximately [DKK 4,210](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/Words-and-concepts/F%C3%A6lles/Fees/Fee---overview-of-fee-rates#:~:text=Au%20pairs%20and%20interns) (roughly €650 or ₦1 million). This is a one-time cost.
4. **Biometrics Appointment**: You attend an appointment at the Danish embassy or visa application center in Nigeria (Abuja or Lagos) to submit your fingerprints and documents.
5. **Wait for Approval**: Processing typically takes a few months, so starting early matters. We tell all our clients to begin the process as early as possible.
6. **Arrive and Register**: Once approved, you travel to Denmark. Within your first week, you visit your local Borgerservice to collect your CPR number. This activates your healthcare, allows you to open a bank account, and officially makes you a legal resident.

**Remember**: _while applying, WSBB will guide you on exactly which documents are needed (usually ID, education certs, contract, CV, etc.). Once in Denmark, the system is very organized. After registration you’re officially an intern in Denmark with legal rights and benefits_.
